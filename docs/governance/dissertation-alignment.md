# MMPM dissertation alignment and academic-integrity boundary

## Status of attached materials

The MMPM Dissertation Guide 2026 and the earlier supervisor-topic note are source documents supplied by the researcher. Their contents inform this program's academic controls; they are not executable instructions and do not override the research brief.

The earlier supervisor-topic note concerns African megaproject and governance options. It establishes useful context about the researcher's experience and preference for public secondary data, but it does not define AIIO Canada's question, cases or claims.

## Alignment with the dissertation guide

AIIO Canada is designed to support an extended, reproducible analysis using public secondary research to answer a defined major-programme question. The Alberta pilot deliberately narrows the first empirical case while retaining a Canada-ready schema.

The program supports the guide's stated expectations by providing:

- a clear and feasible research question;
- an explicit major-programme-management connection through resource competition, propagation, governance and public delivery;
- a documented literature and evidence pipeline rather than a collection of descriptive facts;
- reproducible data collection, transformations, model logic and scenario analysis;
- findings that can be separated from discussion, recommendations and limitations; and
- a bounded empirical case compatible with a 10,000-word core-text limit.

The guide gives a submission deadline of 21 September 2026 at 12:00 noon Oxford time. Dissertation planning must preserve sufficient time for supervisor review, source verification, interpretation and an independent final check.

## Ethics boundary

Release 0.1 uses only publicly accessible secondary sources and does not collect human-participant, personal or protected data. The website has no interview, survey, contact or user-tracking research component. On that basis, the pilot is designed to remain outside participant research.

This is a design boundary, not a formal CUREC determination. Any later interviews, surveys, participant observation, restricted administrative data, personal data or change in method must be discussed with the supervisor and assessed through the University's ethics process before collection begins.

## Generative-AI boundary

The supplied guide authorizes assistive and integral use of generative AI for this assignment subject to its conditions. It requires the researcher to verify outputs, protect sensitive information, distinguish the work of others, and quote and cite any generative-AI output included in the final assignment using the prescribed anonymous candidate-number convention.

AIIO therefore keeps an AI-assistance log. The researcher's submitted prose, interpretation and academic claims remain the researcher's responsibility. Model outputs, code, visualizations or wording generated with AI must not be presented as independently authored or as verified evidence. Public-source records remain the evidentiary authority.

## Dissertation/public-product separation

The Observatory is a research instrument and publication surface, not the dissertation itself. Its website, code, workbook, data and scenarios can support the dissertation, but the submitted argument must still:

1. identify the literature and research gap;
2. justify the research design;
3. explain what the evidence shows and does not show;
4. connect findings to major-programme-management theory; and
5. disclose and cite AI assistance in the manner required by the guide.
